## Based on the bargaining survey, most members would be largely happy with just keeping our contract language as is, making it unlikely that we would have the collective strength needed to get many more protections and benefits in the next contract. Why shouldn't I consider voting for other candidates because it seems they are heavily folding this assessment into their organizing vision.

### How my perspective differs.

**I believe that interpretation of the bargaining survey results neglects much of the specific comments left in the survey, overlooks the past three years of grievances and the parts of the contract we still have to fight for today.** It has become clear in our organizing through the survey, and in the CAt research groups than on issues surrounding international worker protections, health care and accessibility, and funding security that Northwestern has been able to take advantage of related articles of the contract to harm graduate workers. **I also believe from personal experience speaking with many current and incoming workers that these issues are incredibly important to our membership across the university and we can easily gain momentum from them towards the collective strength needed for change.**

### Do you think too much optimism about what we could get in the next contract might be bringing hopes up in only a minority of members without enough power to back it up in time for bargaining?

**This is where listening to our membership and empowering them to speak up about the issues they are about becomes extremely important. The more we organize about the issues membership has indicated to us the more likely that optimism will become a reality.** At the same time we need to be realistic and emphasize that the contract fight is a negotiation. We need to be asking more specific questions to our members, for example rather than just know what level of priority an issue is we need to know what they would be willing to compromise on in order to save that specific issue. Transparent and open bargaining sessions will also go a long way in helping our membership understand and trust this negotiation process. **I want us to be hopeful because hope is powerful, but I also want to make sure we are prepared to deliver on key contract protections in a worse case scenario where Northwestern tries to take away every article we have in our current contract. In other words we need to be optimistic and hope for the best because if we never ask for something we will never get it but we also need to be ready to fight to keep every last article in the contract and know the difference between what our members want and what they cannot survive without.**

### Isn’t this likely to lead to more pain and fracturing in our union down the line? 

**That is a very realistic concern. If we as a bargaining committee aren’t clear about our priorities going in and if we don't give membership an explanation for whatever may occur during bargaining then we are sowing the seeds of a divide.** If as leadership we want to have the power of our membership behind us for collective bargaining we need to do everything we can to build their trust in us when we sit at the bargaining table. One of the ways I believe we could do this is with open bargaining. **Imagine how much understanding membership would have of the contract fight if we let them witness it. If you could watch bargaining sessions then submit a form after with comments on specific articles or what was discussed we as a bargaining committee would know how membership felt before it comes to final contract ratification.** Members would be able to trust us because we would be visibly accountable to them during bargaining, helping prevent pain and fracturing in our union down the line.

### Isn’t it arguably more democratic to heed the sentiment from members that they mostly just want the status quo and just focus on the likely incredibly hard battle of just keeping what we have? 

Like I mentioned before, **if we never ask for better we will never get anything better.** It will be an incredibly hard battle but if keeping what we have now is our bare minimum goal then we must ask for more in our contract, for higher pay, for more protections for international workers, for better healthcare, and we must show that we are willing to fight for a better contract than we have now. **We must aim higher because this shows Northwestern that we want better, and then keeping our current status quo becomes the compromise. However if we only ever fight for what we currently have we will be forced to make compromises and end up losing the status quo. We must remember that bargaining is a negotiation and that we have collective bargaining power because Northwestern cannot survive without its graduate workers, and so we must aim higher in the hopes of getting a better contract but prepare for the hard battle that may mean we only keep what's in our current contract.**
