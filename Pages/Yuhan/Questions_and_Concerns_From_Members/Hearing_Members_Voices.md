## There has been significant discussion, both during the last contract cycle and leading into the current one, about how members’ voices should be incorporated into the bargaining process. What is your vision for how members can learn about and provide feedback into the course of bargaining? Will the opinions of those in and out of the BU be considered differently? What does rank-and-file democracy look like between the time that the BC is elected and the contact is voted on?

- Weekly bargaining updates: proposals, admin responses, movement, and unresolved issues.
- Short issue-specific surveys before major bargaining decisions.
- Stewards/DCSs bring department-level feedback directly back to the BC.
- Town halls at major turning points, especially before dropping or trading major demands.
- Anonymous feedback option for members who do not want to speak publicly.
- Targeted outreach to less-engaged departments/groups, not just people already active in union spaces.
- BU members have decision-making priority; non-BU workers can still provide relevant input and context.
- Publish what we heard from members so surveys and meetings do not feel like a black box.

These are methods for these things, and as leaders, it’s important to keep up with them and make sure the people responsible are doing the same. It’s much easier to add meeting notes to the folder as you go than to sort through a big pile of documents later.

But most importantly, I don’t think our role should just be keeping members informed and in the loop. We also need to get people excited, encourage them to take action, and support them in fighting for what they believe in. We should give people a platform to speak up and take initiative. It’s always more powerful when people feel like they are making things happen, rather than just hearing about what has already happened.
