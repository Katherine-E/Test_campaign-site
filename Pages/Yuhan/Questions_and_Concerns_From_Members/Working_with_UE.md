## Experienced UE negotiators like Kim Lawson provided instrumental guidance to the first BC, helping us win an exceptionally strong first contract (especially compared to the first contracts of peer unions like UIC). What’s so wrong about voting for other candidates who have a strong track record of heeding advice from UE organizers and/or GEB?

To be honest, I don’t think there’s anything wrong with voting for any candidate. Voting for candidate A doesn’t mean candidate B is necessarily incompetent. People come in with different beliefs and priorities, and candidates are always going to be different. It’s our job to explain what we believe in clearly, and may the person who best represents what most members want win.

I’ve talked with a lot of organizers who were involved in the last contract fight, and I do think saying “UE helped us win an exceptionally strong first contract” can be a little misleading if it leaves out the enormous amount of work done by our own local organizers. UE staff have provided important guidance and resources, but our members and organizers were the ones who built the campaign, made decisions, mobilized workers, and fought for that contract.

I think any advice (or even instruction) that comes from outside should remain neutral. Yes, we are a UE local, but first and foremost, NUGW is a member-run union. We should always believe that our members know best what they want and need, and that our own workers know best how to fight for those things.

I absolutely agree that we need to stand in solidarity with existing groups on campus, like postdocs, undergraduates, non-tenure-track faculty, etc., and make full use of  all the support and resources available to us during a contract fight, including help from UE staff. But all of that needs to be built on the basis that WE are taking the initiative and ownership, that we represent the rights and needs of graduate workers in our local, and that this principle is respected by everyone we work with.
