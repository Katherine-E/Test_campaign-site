## The constitutional duties of the VP for Membership includes spearheading membership enforcement, which usually requires a lot of listwork and other work that isn’t as “glamorous”/member-facing or immediately gratifying as organizing/activism. Could you speak to any experiences or aptitude you have that would make you well-suited for that type of work?

I have experience organizing and leading multi-day backpacking trips, where my responsibilities extended well beyond leading the group in the field. I recruited and screened participants, managed surveys and spreadsheets, coordinated logistics, planned routes and itineraries, and prepared the group before departure.

What I learned from that experience is that I genuinely enjoy both sides of organizing: the member-facing work and the less visible operational work that makes everything else possible. I am energized by working directly with people, but I also like working with data and information like keeping lists accurate, identifying patterns, following up systematically, and making sure no one falls through the cracks.

I have used surveys, spreadsheets, and other tracking systems extensively in both trip organizing and research. For me, those tools are not just administrative tasks; they are a way to understand a group at scale. In a leadership role, it is not realistic to know every member equally well through one-on-one interaction, so good membership data can provide a broader picture of who our members are, where engagement may be dropping off, and where follow-up is needed.

That combination of enjoying direct organizing while also being comfortable with detailed, repetitive, behind-the-scenes work is one reason I think I would be well-suited for membership enforcement.
