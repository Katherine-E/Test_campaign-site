## I haven’t seen you very much before, what has your organizing experience been? Why do you feel you can do this job with only ~5 months organizing experience and no prior EB role? Especially since your opponent is known to be an experienced organizer?

If we haven’t met or talked much yet, let’s change that!!

I’ve been organizing through DIAS around research, international student orientation, the International Support Fund, and bringing in new international members/organizers while being mindful of their safety. I’ve also made an effort to talk with people leading different areas of union work so I have a good sense of what’s happening beyond just my own projects

I may be newer to union organizing, but I’m not new to leadership. I’ve led other communities and organized multi-day backpacking trips from start to finish which includes recruiting and screening people, planning logistics, managing information, and leading the group in the field (already explained above).

I also think being relatively new gives me a useful perspective. My program didn’t have a local steward, so for a long time I experienced the union as a rank-and-file member. When I first got involved, I remember getting sent a ton of documents and folders and thinking, “There has to be an easier way to understand all of this.” I want to make information and onboarding much more accessible, especially for members who are interested but don’t already know how everything works.

And VP isn’t a role that stands alone. I’m running as part of a team with Ed and Katherine, who are both respected and experienced organizers, and Yara will be supporting me throughout the transition. We also have many experienced organizers, including people who played major roles in the last contract fight, working alongside us. I plan to listen carefully, learn from that experience, and, honestly, pick everyone’s brains whenever I can lol. I don’t think good leadership means already knowing everything; it means knowing how to bring people together, listen, learn quickly, and make good use of the experience around you.
