## How will you handle backdues?

By our contract, all members, whether in unit or out of unit, are required to pay dues to NUGW. This is a phenomenal protection that allows our organizing power to thrive while providing all members a say in our union’s decision making. Handling backdues has been a consistent and difficult conversation with stewards and general members alike. Mishandling backdues can cause a member to feel fear and distrust that our union has their best interest at heart. This is why it is vital to approach every conversation with workers who owe backdues with patience and empathy. As the president, I will work hand in hand with the VP of Membership, the CCS, and our steward network to work on organizing plans to reach all workers who owe backdues

To put it plainly, we cannot take shortcuts with backdues enforcement. We all need to put in consistent effort to lay out why we collect dues, what our dues go on to fund, and most importantly, how dues protect us and our coworkers. These conversations should preferably be conducted in person, one-on-one with our coworker to address fears and misconceptions right then and there. 

The first step in handling backdues is preventing them from accruing in the first place. This comes from an active effort of reaching all workers who have yet to sign cards. We have been consistently working on membership drive and should still be placing heavy emphasis on this practice!

The next step is developing a concrete plan of how to reach members who have slipped through the cracks. This plan should involve heavy steward involvement, which also means that stewards should be bought into the plan as well. Therefore, an organizing plan for reaching these members should be subject to a GMM vote to ensure that all of us are on the same page. 

Following through with the plan, we must be having consistent and frequent one-on-one conversations with these workers, led with an empathetic lens. In my time, I have found that a majority of members not only want to pay dues, but are excited about the fact that their money is going to a great cause. I believe that we need to approach these workers with that respect and understanding instead of assuming that all those who owe backdues just “don’t want to pay.” There will be cases in which a worker may feel that way, but by assuming all workers who owe back dues just “don’t want to pay dues,” we risk alienating an ally and, by proxy, this vital contract protection in general.
