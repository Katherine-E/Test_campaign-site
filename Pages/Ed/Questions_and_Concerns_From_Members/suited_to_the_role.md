## The President role is one of the most difficult and intense roles of the LEB and has a steep learning curve. Why do you think you’d be better-suited for the role than the incumbent? This is a bargaining year. Why would I take a risk on someone new if the incumbent is already doing a fine job?


One of the largest duties of the president is to ensure that all members of our union feel heard and understood. Nearly all of the time, this comes from one-on-one conversations with stewards and general members across all departments to truly understand how issues affect all workers. From these conversations, the president has the ability to alleviate fear and inspire the stewards and members to take action on their own. 

In my time as DCS, I encouraged many local stewards to pursue grievances and actions that became crucial for the organizing of our upcoming contract. For instance, I heavily encouraged many stewards in DIAS to pursue the passport renewal grievance, which made it all the way to Level 3 and won! The majority of the work was not done by me, but by giving guidance and inspiring hope into our local organizers. **By passing along my knowledge and experience to newer organizers, we were able to work together to win this grievance. In this way, I was able to play a crucial role in expanding this protection from our contract and I will continue to assist and support newer organizers so that we can have a strong union of confident organizers.**

I also assisted our local organizers in the expansion of the Accessibility and Healthcare working group, which has done the excellent work of organizing around our current and future healthcare protections. This work has done a tremendous amount of good for our union, including “Know Your Healthcare” workshops that have been useful for members across the university. This working group has also paved the way to enshrine dependent healthcare into our contract for our parents and caregivers. I am not the lead of this working group. Instead, I have been a participant who only hoped to see that others were becoming more involved.

I have also been at the grievance table for my workers many times as both DCS and as a local steward. I have won fights on recognition of internal fellows (including, but not limited to, Presidential and John M Nicholson Fellows) and continue to fight for TAs in the math department who have actively been denied contract protections, such as their protection to work during normal work hours. I have fought for workers who have been threatened with academic discipline and discharge. While some of these fights were ultimately unsuccessful, I continue to learn from these experiences on how we can continue forcing the university to honor the protections we mutually agreed upon. 

For years, I have regularly attended the Organizing Committee, Contract Action Team, and GMMs to learn from other organizers and take action for myself. I have admittedly missed some of these meetings that I wish I had not, but I have always been committed to staying informed through meeting minutes and conversations with peers and fellow organizers. 

I believe that a lot of our current organizing power has been focused on telling a steward or member to do something as opposed to asking them if they are willing to. In the upcoming contract fight, I envision a union where we truly understand members’ fears and concerns with the potential of a strike. We need to deeply understand these workers so that they feel confident in said strike. Without the buy-in of membership, we do not have the organizing power we need. To get that buy-in, we cannot simply tell the members to strike, we must ask why they would not and show them that the people of this university have the true power. I believe that I have the capability of fostering such an environment for our union.

Most of all, I believe I am best for this role as I have an unblinding faith in my fellow organizers and general membership that will never falter. All of us are a part of this union for a reason: we all work to protect each other. That means that the work we do is done together. I have trust that all of us want to put in the work to ensure this union runs properly.  Much of the duty of the president is delegation and ensuring that the work gets done.
