## Based on the bargaining survey, most members would be largely happy with just keeping our contract language as is, making it unlikely that we would have the collective strength needed to get many more protections and benefits in the next contract. Optimism is great but do you think too much optimism about what we could get in the next contract might be bringing hopes up in only a minority of members without enough power to back it up in time for bargaining? Isn’t this likely to lead to more pain and fracturing in our union down the line? Isn’t it arguably more democratic to heed the sentiment from members that they mostly just want the status quo and just focus on the likely incredibly hard battle of just keeping what we have? 

Our current contract IS great! The wins of the previous bargaining committee are excellent and I want to ensure that any decision we make will still allow us to protect said wins. However, this does not mean our contract is perfect. For instance, many of our parents feel that the cost of their children’s healthcare is far too high on NU-SHIP. This is something that the University could easily pay for, but they refuse to because the University knows that it can continue to divide us further. As our Accessibility and Healthcare working group has found, parents and caregivers make up only around 2% of the grad student population. But I have and will continue to ask, does that mean we should allow the university to treat them unfairly? Yes, a majority of grad workers are not parents. But, what if we extend that same argument to other inequitably supported groups, do we stop caring for small fractions of the population purely because they aren’t the majority? Absolutely not! The truth is, the University picks on said groups across the University to instill fear that there is no solution for them, which is why we need to speak up for those who the University attempts to silence. This is a win that I do believe is achievable, and the University will try to do everything to make it seem that it is not. But by allowing this thought process to continue, we make it so the University controls the narrative and not us.

Being hopeful is not mutually exclusive with being realistic. Yes, the University will most likely attack many of our contract protections. Yes, the University will most likely try and divide us again. Yes, the funding climate in academia right now is atrocious. These are real fears that I and many others have. But these fears cannot stifle us. Fear is a tool used by the University to lull us into inaction. They do this because the University feels that they have power over us. What the University fails to recognize is that they do not. 

This University works only because the grad workers, staff, and postdocs allow it to. What happens to a class if there is no grad TA for that course? What happens to the University’s grants if there is no grad RA to do the actual day-to-day research? There is a reason why the NLRB decided that PhD students can be labelled as workers, and that is because WE keep these Universities afloat. We have the power at this University, and we cannot let the University strip this power away through these fears.
This contract fight will be a hard battle, just as the previous contract was and just as all our future contracts will be. But this is a hard battle that we can, and must, win together. By allowing the University to instill this fear into us, we lose this contract fight before we even begin. If we allow the University to diminish our hopes of what could be, we will lose what we already have.

At the end of the day, a new contract can be quite exciting! It is the time in which we can yell at the University and say “I care for my fellow grad workers!” It is the time for us to not only say that we want more protections, but DEMAND that we NEED more protections. Let’s say the University comes after our dental and vision care, do we sit and allow them to take it away? NO! We stand up and fight for it! This fight is a completely different fight, and we need to be prepared for all the stones the University will throw while continuing to push for more.
Hope is not the reason why splits occur, it is hope that heals a fractured psyche. There is a better world out there, and we must fight for it.
