## There has been significant discussion, both during the last contract cycle and leading into the current one, about how members’ voices should be incorporated into the bargaining process. What is your vision for how members can learn about and provide feedback into the course of bargaining? Will the opinions of those in and out of the BU be considered differently? What does rank-and-file democracy look like between the time that the BC is elected and the contact is voted on?

I believe that member’s voices should be at the forefront of the bargaining process. Part of this comes from direct communication to and from the bargaining committee in consistent bargaining updates and an easily accessible bargaining tracker. While these tools are exceptionally helpful to learn about what is happening at the bargaining table, we must also allow for members to be involved in the drafting and editing of proposed articles. By allowing members to assist in the drafting process, we both show that the BC is acting in accordance with members’ wishes and members develop a stronger bond to the contract itself. To paraphrase Alexandra Bradburry, there are no “ins and outs” if everyone feels like they were “in” from the start.

To reach all members, we must use our steward network to ask for targeted feedback on specific proposals both before general membership meetings and before the BC presents said proposals at the bargaining table. Once the stewards learn what membership wants, they should bring their findings directly to the BC where the proposal will then be edited in accordance with members’ wishes. These proposals could be shown to membership through outreach such as walkthroughs, anonymous feedback forms, town halls, suggesting access on copies of proposal documents, or a combination of everything before.

Outside of drafting proposal documents, members can also decide whether to have bargaining sessions open to general members, which can be an extremely powerful tool for many reasons:

1) Open bargaining shows members that the BC is acting on their behalf and shows the BC that the members have their backs, and in doing so we build solidarity
2) Open bargaining allows members to see the university’s shady tactics directly, and in doing so we build our power
3) Open bargaining shows the university that we all are watching, and in doing so we show our strength

However, the university could become less likely or unwilling to speak on compromises at the bargaining table if this occurs for every session. In contrast, this also means that the university may be less willing to attempt a ruthless attack on our contract, which could be very vital in this current political climate. 

Open bargaining could also cause bargaining to become a slower process, which in our much shorter bargaining timeline could become quite the hassle. With more people in the room, there will be more voices that can be heard. However, just as our GMMs follow Robert’s Rules of Order, we can establish similar practices during an open bargaining session to allow for proper time management. 

With all this being said, I am by no means an expert on this topic either, and I will push for a more fruitful discussion with BC and membership should be had before we begin bargaining. 

In terms of bargaining unit status, we are forced into difficult conversations based on the bad practices of Northwestern University. Famously, the university tends to overwhelmingly consider Humanities and Social Studies workers out of the bargaining unit while STEM workers tend to be considered in unit. This is completely bad practice from the university and should be treated as such. All grad workers at Northwestern are working for Northwestern and deserve to be protected by our contract. We must not forget that this distinction only exists because the university arbitrarily decided to place people off the bargaining unit and we must continue to fight to include more workers to be formally recognized.

To me, the members must feel like they have control over the contract to feel like it is truly theirs. By doing extended outreach to members both in and out of the grievance table, we can maintain our current protections AND fight for protections that are even greater!
