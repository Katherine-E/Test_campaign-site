## You claim to like “healthy disagreement” and a bottom up members based approach to organizing, but how will you actually get things done? What if there is so much discussion about a topic that no choice can be made? In that case, isn’t it better to give the EB a greater amount of authority to make a decision and keep things running?

Before I answer, I do want to clarify what I mean by “healthy disagreement.” By healthy disagreements, I do not mean allowing for bigotry to foster. Bigotry to me is not a “healthy disagreement” and should not be tolerated or allowed in our spaces. A healthy disagreement to me is one in which both parties hold respect for the other and care for what the other has to say. These disagreements can look like asking for a change of language in a proposal or discussions on how to best run our local. These disagreements do not look like name calling, yelling, general acts of bigotry, or assuming that the other worker is disagreeing in bad faith.

With that out of the way, growth only exists when one leaves an area of comfort. As graduate workers, we know full well that learning something new requires you to go against the standard literature and look at others’ work in a new light. Our fields thrive on disruptions, because in these disruptions we learn something new. For example, cool example from physics to get the people pumped. 

Our organizing power comes from having disagreements with coworkers, and showing Northwestern that these disagreements cannot and will not change the fact that we are stronger together. Our organizing power comes from learning about others’ experiences and navigating conflict together. In having healthy disagreements, and by that I mean disagreements over what policy is best to pass in our local (for example), and by truly listening to one another with an empathetic ear, we can grow alongside one another instead of against. Our organizing power comes from making informed decisions WITH one another.

Full discussions on topics are extremely important for all members to make well-informed decisions for the local. While these discussions may be tricky to navigate, I believe that when presented with all of the information in a decision, a majority of members will make the same decision that any EB member would. The duty of the president is to facilitate these decisions, such that all voices are heard and membership can make this well-informed decision. If no decision gets made, it is because the discussions were not made beforehand. If further discussion on a topic is needed, then that discussion should continue.

For our union to remain strong, members need to own the decisions being made. Giving EB a greater amount of authority may seem appealing, after all we voted them in, but the EB is still a small population in comparison to the entire local. The executive board’s role in our union is not to alleviate the decision making capabilities from general members. Instead, the executive board’s role is to communicate with members about what the people across our university desire to see and act on that. This only happens if the president, the executive board, and general members are willing to have a discussion where members feel comfortable to voice their opinion.
