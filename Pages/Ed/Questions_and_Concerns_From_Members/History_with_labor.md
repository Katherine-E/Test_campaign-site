## Other candidates seem like they would be a great President again because they have a lot of passion for unions, including reading several works on labor history in their spare time to have a strong foundation on labor theory and NUGW-UE’s broader context in the labor movement. I’m worried about the idea of voting for a President who doesn’t have a deep knowledge of our labor history.

There are many ways in which one can learn how to organize, including reading theory. However, theory and application are two vastly different skill sets. As a physics PhD worker, I have learned the theories of quantum mechanics, but that doesn’t inherently imply that I can operate a scanning tunneling microscope. In my own experience organizing, I have learned from some of our best organizers and have experienced first hand how to effectively alleviate people’s fears and frustrations of organizing and turning that into actionable items. Alongside that, I do agree that a bit of knowledge on labor theory does help organizers develop guiding principles for which they can stand by, which is why I do read labor theory in my free time as well. In terms of NUGW-UE’s broader context in the labor movement, we have the power to shape this story together through both communications in our union and our sibling locals such as Chicago GSU. I also know that in places where I may falter (such as a deep, sophisticated study of the labor movement), I can rely on my fellow organizers who actively study labor movements for their PhD to bring that knowledge and context to the forefront of our struggle. Labor history and knowledge is built on the work of millions who have come before me, which is why I plan on having consistent communications with organizers across our local and at other locals to develop new paths forward in solidarity.
